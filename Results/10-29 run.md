## Where the skill comes from

Windows are split by the rain that fell between each window's last hour and its target. That rain is used for
scoring only; it is never an input.

| | windows | share of last value's squared error | RMSE skill | MAE skill | model bias | last value bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| test, no gap rain (< 2 mm) | 6,506 (75%) | 16% | **+39.3%** | **+49.2%** | +0.0045 | +0.0108 |
| test, gap rain (≥ 2 mm) | 2,183 (25%) | 84% | −4.5% | −0.9% | −0.0338 | −0.0266 |
| val, no gap rain | 7,067 (82%) | 14% | **+46.0%** | **+45.9%** | +0.0016 | +0.0081 |
| val, gap rain | 1,598 (18%) | 86% | +0.4% | +3.6% | −0.0415 | −0.0376 |

- **The model has learned the drydown.** When no rain comes, it roughly halves last value's error. Its high bias
  in those windows (+0.0045 on test) is less than half of last value's (+0.0108). The old "predictions sit too
  wet" problem is mostly gone. In the plots, the model line runs below the last-value line through every long
  drydown: March, June, August, September, October.
- **The rain it cannot see is what caps RMSE.** In the 25% of windows with gap rain, which hold 84% of the squared
  error, the model is slightly *worse* than last value on test (−4.5% RMSE). It forecasts the drydown, so when rain
  does come it ends up further below the actual value than last value does (bias −0.034 vs −0.027). That is the
  cost of a loss that follows the typical outcome. It is why test RMSE skill is only +1.4% while MAE skill is +23%,
  and why the overall bias is negative (−0.005, too dry).

## Remaining problems

1. **False rises still happen.** On test, the model forecast a rise of more than 0.02 in 88 windows; in 74 of them
   (84%) `SWC_20` rose by less than 0.005. On validation it was 41 of 59. That is under 1% of windows, and much
   smaller than the +0.05 to +0.08 spikes of the earlier notebooks. In the monthly plots they cluster in about
   four events: mid-May, mid-June (forecast up to 0.34 while the actual stayed at about 0.27), mid-July and around
   9 September. Each looks like rain inside the input window that never reached 20 cm.
2. **Rises are still late.** Every jump caused by rain in the gap shows up in the forecast 72 hours later, exactly
   like last value. This cannot be fixed without a rain forecast as input.
3. **Low-signal months are slightly worse.** The model beats last value on MAE in 9 of 12 months, ties in April,
   and loses in November and December. In those two months `SWC_20` sits flat near 0.20 (last value MAE only
   0.0021–0.0025) and the model adds ±0.005 of wiggle (e.g. a dip to 0.19 around 24 November). On RMSE it loses in
   April, July (0.0609 vs 0.0571, the false rise plus several gap-rain storms), November and December.

Largest monthly MAE gains: August 0.0057 vs 0.0143 (−60%), October 0.0014 vs 0.0031 (−55%), March 0.0042 vs
0.0070 (−40%), June 0.0321 vs 0.0434 (−26%).

## CV check (TimeSeriesSplit: 2019 → 2020, 2019–2020 → 2021)

| variant | RMSE skill (fold 1 / fold 2) | MAE skill (fold 1 / fold 2) | beats last value everywhere | best epochs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 earlier inputs, MSE | +3.7% / +5.4% | +4.5% / +11.3% | **yes** | 22 / 17 |
| 2 earlier inputs, Huber | +0.6% / −0.3% | +13.6% / +12.3% | no | 5 / 8 |
| 11 engineered, MSE | +3.6% / +2.1% | +8.6% / −5.7% | no | 10 / 1 |
| 11 engineered, Huber (final) | −2.2% / −1.3% | +9.4% / +13.8% | no | 2 / 9 |

- **Loss decides the RMSE/MAE trade-off.** MSE variants win on RMSE and Huber variants win on MAE, on both folds.
  Huber variants are too dry (fold bias −0.005 to −0.008).
- **The engineered features did not help in CV.** With 1–2 training years, 11 features is no better than 2 on MAE
  and worse on RMSE. The 11-feature Huber model stopped at epoch 2 in fold 1, which trains on 2019 only, so it
  overfits fast with that little data.
- **The final model (3 training years) did much better than its CV folds** (MAE skill +23% on 2022 and 2024
  against +9–14% in CV). This run **cannot say how much of that comes from the features and how much from the
  extra training year**, because no 2-input model was trained on 2019–2021 and scored on 2022 and 2024. This is
  the main open question.

## Training

27 epochs run, best epoch 17. Earlier runs stopped with best epochs of 3 and 7, so the longer budget is being used.
Training loss falls steadily (0.000715 → 0.000515, −28%). Validation loss is noisy and improves only about 8%
(0.000744 at epoch 1 → 0.000683 at epoch 17), with a spike at epoch 9. After about epoch 10 the model improves on
training data much more than on 2022, so it is starting to overfit.

## Next steps, in order

1. **Isolate the feature effect.** Train "2 earlier inputs, Huber" on 2019–2021 and score it on 2022 and 2024
   against this run. If it matches, the gain is the loss and the third training year, not the features.
2. **Trade a little MAE for RMSE.** Try a larger Huber δ (e.g. 0.01–0.02, between Huber 0.005 and MSE), checking
   the gap-rain rows of the breakdown table.
3. **Look at the ~4 false-rise events** (rain amount, `SWC_5` response, antecedent wetness) before adding any
   feature for them.
4. **Overfitting after about epoch 10:** dropout or weight decay is an option, if the fixed config may change.
5. **Keep the outputs:** `OUTPUT_DIR` wrote to the Colab runtime, so the CSVs and JSON vanish when it shuts down.
